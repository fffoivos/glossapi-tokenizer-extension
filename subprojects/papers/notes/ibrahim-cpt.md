# Simple and Scalable Strategies to Continually Pre-train Large Language Models

**Source:** arXiv:2403.08763  |  **Topic:** LR schedule (CPT)  |  `txt/ibrahim-cpt.txt`, `pdf/ibrahim-cpt.pdf`

**TL;DR.** Empirical study (405M and 10B decoder-only LLMs, 200B+ new tokens) showing that LR re-warming + re-decaying + a small amount of replay of prior data matches full re-training-from-scratch on the union of all data, at a fraction of the compute.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Core recipe: when continuing from a checkpoint whose LR was cosine-decayed to a small ηmin, (1) re-warm the LR back up toward ηmax, (2) re-decay it with cosine annealing to ~0.1·ηmax over the new token budget, and (3) mix in a small fraction of replay of the previous dataset. Combined, this matches the union-trained (full re-train) baseline on final loss and average eval (abstract l.39-42; contributions 1-3 l.116-228; Fig 1 l.211-219).
- Re-warming + re-decaying is NECESSARY for adaptation: constant-ηmin continuation adapts the least and re-warming/re-decaying 'adapt better to the new dataset by a significant margin for both distribution shifts' (Fig 4, l.1002-1009; summary l.1037, l.1050).
- Peak-LR dial (the adaptation-vs-forgetting trade-off, claim 2): 'Decreasing the schedule's maximum learning rate can help reduce forgetting, whereas increasing it can improve adaptation' (rule of thumb, l.266-267). Important nuance the dial framing hides: among re-warming models these are SMALL differences, and higher ηmax raises BOTH adaptation AND forgetting together (they co-move, not a clean trade) -- 'higher values of ηmax lead to more forgetting and more adaptation while the opposite is true for lower values' (l.1038-1040, l.1051).
- Concrete LR grid tested at 405M: pre-training ηmax = 3e-4, ηmin = 3e-5 (= 0.1·ηmax). Re-warm targets swept were 1.5e-4 (half), 3e-4 (same), 6e-4 (double); cosine-decay to 0.1·ηmax; linear warmup = 1% of iterations (l.1004-1006, l.1026-1028). Warmup DURATION (0%-2%) was found not to materially affect forgetting/adaptation (l.860-861, l.1053).
- Replay numbers: even 1% replay 'significantly reduces forgetting' at little adaptation cost; 1/5/10% have little downstream cost, but 50% (extreme) noticeably hurts adaptation to new data (l.1231-1239). Replay is 'compute-equivalent' -- replay tokens REPLACE new-data tokens within a fixed budget, not added on top (Sec 4.2, l.472-490).
- Shift strength sets the replay dose: weak shift (English->English, Pile->SlimPajama) used 5% replay; strong shift (English->German) used 25% replay. Stronger shift exacerbates both forgetting and adaptation and 'requires more replay to mitigate forgetting to the same extent' (l.216-217, l.1222, l.1337-1338, l.1356).
- Scale & generality: the weak-shift recipe (re-warm/re-decay + 5% replay) matches the re-train baseline at BOTH 405M and 10B (l.46-48, l.1648, l.1812-1815). Third parties replicated at larger scale: Glorioso et al. (Zamba) used 60% replay over a 50B decay phase; DeepSeek-V2 used 30% replay over 6T tokens with a non-decayed starting checkpoint (l.281-293).
- Optimizer/forgetting caveat: re-warming itself can CAUSE optimization-related forgetting; the paper proposes 'infinite LR schedules' (transition to a high constant LR across tasks, with a final exponential decay) as an alternative that avoids re-warming between tasks and avoids committing to a fixed token budget (l.229-231, l.268-272, Sec 7). This is a hedge the simple cosine recipe does not eliminate.
- Setting boundary: results are validated for large continual pre-training (datasets Di >= ~100B tokens, N>=2 sequential phases), decoder-only transformers, English/German. NOT a fine-tuning/instruction-tuning or small-domain-adaptation study (l.241-243, l.81-83).

Use it to justify the three-part recipe: re-warm the Apertus LR back up toward its pre-training ηmax, cosine re-decay to ~0.1·ηmax over the Greek token budget, and include compute-equivalent replay of Apertus's original (multilingual/English) pre-training mix to bound forgetting. For the peak-LR setting, cite it for the adaptation-vs-forgetting dial but record the caveat that the effect among re-warming runs is small and that higher peak LR buys adaptation at the cost of more forgetting (which replay then offsets) -- do NOT present higher peak LR as a free adaptation win. Two strong cautions for our context: (1) the English/German results give shift-dependent replay doses (5% weak / 25% strong); an English-dominant base adapting to Greek is plausibly a moderate-to-strong shift, so default toward the higher end of the replay range and treat the exact fraction as a sweep parameter, not a number to copy.
