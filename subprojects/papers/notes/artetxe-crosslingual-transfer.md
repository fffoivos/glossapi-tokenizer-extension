# On the Cross-lingual Transferability of Monolingual Representations (Artetxe, Ruder, Yogatama; arXiv:1910.11856)

**Source:** arXiv:1910.11856  |  **Topic:** New-token init  |  `txt/artetxe-crosslingual-transfer.txt`, `pdf/artetxe-crosslingual-transfer.pdf`

**TL;DR.** Shows that a monolingual transformer (BERT) can be transferred to a new language by freezing the entire transformer body and learning only a fresh token-embedding matrix for the new language via MLM, reaching results competitive with jointly-trained multilingual BERT on cross-lingual classification and QA.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Core recipe (MONOTRANS, 4 steps, §2): (1) pre-train monolingual BERT in L1 with MLM+NSP; (2) transfer to L2 by learning NEW token embeddings while FREEZING the transformer body, same MLM+NSP objective on an L2 monolingual corpus; (3) fine-tune the body on an L1 downstream task with L1 embeddings frozen; (4) zero-shot transfer by swapping in the L2 embeddings. Direct quote (abstract): 'transfer it to a new language by learning a new embedding matrix with the same masked language modeling objective—freezing parameters of all other layers.'
- This demonstrates body-frozen embedding relearning is SUFFICIENT to make a monolingual model work in a new language: 'M ONOT RANS is competitive with J OINT M ULTI and J OINT PAIR, despite being multilingual at the embedding layer only (i.e. the transformer body is trained' only on L1 (lines 687–688).
- Quantified gap (XNLI, §3.3): the BASIC frozen-body variant is '3.3 points worse on average than its equivalent J OINT PAIR model'; adding language-specific position embeddings + noised fine-tuning 'reduce the gap to only 1.1 points.' So freezing the body works but is NOT free — there is a measurable accuracy cost vs. joint training, partly recoverable with extensions.
- Caveat — adapters / extra L2 capacity help, especially on harder tasks: on XQuAD the monolingual transfer approach is 'made competitive with mBERT by learning second language-specific transformations via adapter modules' (Step 2 adds frozen-body adapters). Adapters 'mostly improve performance, except for low-resource languages such as Urdu, Swahili, Thai, and Greek' (line 640) — a direct caveat for a Greek project.
- Caveat — effective vocabulary size per language is identified as 'an important factor' (contributions list and §6): disjoint per-language 32k vocab (J OINT PAIR) beats shared vocab; multilingual models with larger vocab 'obtain consistently better results.' Relevant to a tokenizer-extension project sizing the Greek vocab slice.
- Scope: this is a masked-LM (BERT-style, encoder) study with NSP, evaluated on zero-shot transfer (fine-tune in English, test in other languages) over XNLI/MLDoc/PAWS-X/XQuAD. It is NOT a decoder/causal-LM continued-pretraining study and does NOT measure generative LM loss/perplexity in L2.
- Direction of transfer is monolingual-source -> new-language EMBEDDINGS only; the body is never adapted to L2 in the main method. The paper frames this as a deliberate counterpoint to joint multilingual training, not as a production recipe — it shows embeddings CAN carry the lexical adaptation, not that freezing the body is optimal.
- Probing (§5, Table 4): monolingual representations transferred across languages carry SEMANTIC abstractions well (WiC, SCWS) but SYNTACTIC abstractions transfer with larger gaps ('transferring syntactic abstractions is more challenging than semantic abstractions') — a caveat on how much the frozen body really generalizes.

## How we use it
Cite this as the foundational evidence that a deep monolingual transformer body already encodes language-agnostic abstractions, so adapting a model to Greek can be driven largely through the embedding/lexical layer — the conceptual justification for our merge-rule tokenizer extension + embedding relearning. Use it to support 'new-language embeddings on a fixed body work,' but frame our setting as STRICTLY MORE FAVORABLE than the paper's: we run continued pretraining on Apertus-8B (a causal decoder), so unlike MONOTRANS we also let the body adapt to Greek rather than freezing it — the paper is a lower bound, establishing the body need not be retrained from scratch, not a mandate to freeze it. Carry three caveats into any citation: (a) frozen-body embedding-only transfer left a residual gap (1.1–3.3 pts on XNLI) vs joint training, so embeddings alone are not free; (b) results are BERT/MLM/zero-shot-classification, not decoder-LM perplexity, so it is analogical support; (c) the paper flags Greek by name as a low-resource case where the adapter extension failed to help, and that effective per-language vocabulary size matters — both directly relevant to sizing our Greek vocab slice and to expecting Greek to be a harder-than-average target. Do NOT cite this paper to justify freezing the Apertus body during CPT; it supports the opposite framing (the body generalizes well enough that lexical adaptation is the load-bearing piece).
