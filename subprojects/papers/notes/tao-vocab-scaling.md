# Scaling Laws with Vocabulary: Larger Models Deserve Larger Vocabularies (Tao et al., NeurIPS 2024, arXiv:2407.13623)

**Source:** arXiv:2407.13623  |  **Topic:** Vocabulary cutoff  |  `txt/tao-vocab-scaling.txt`, `pdf/tao-vocab-scaling.pdf`

**TL;DR.** A compute-optimal scaling-law study showing that the optimal vocabulary size grows with model/compute budget (so most LLMs under-size their vocab) — derived for monolingual English models trained from scratch, NOT for multilingual continual-pretraining vocab extension.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Central thesis is the OPPOSITE of cutting vocab: optimal vocabulary GROWS with model size/compute. Abstract predicts Llama2-70B should have used >=216K (7x its 32K); raising V from 32K->43K at fixed 2.3e21 FLOPs lifted ARC-Challenge 29.1->32.0 (Table 3, overtraining row).
- Three convergent methods predict V_opt: (A1) IsoFLOPs power-law fit, (A2) derivative of FLOPs w.r.t. V, (A3) parametric Chinchilla-style loss fit. Only Approach 3 handles non-compute-optimal data allocation (under/overtraining).
- Table 1 compute-optimal V_opt predictions (data scaled with params): 3B->~37-43K, 7B->~60-67K, 13B->~81-91K, 30B->~142-154K, 70B->~212-231K. An 8B model interpolates to roughly 62-70K total vocab. The SMALLEST V_opt anywhere in Table 1 is ~37K (for a 3B model).
- Mechanism for why a finite optimum exists (Sec 3, eq. context lines 305-310): at small V, fertility gains dominate; at large V the extra embedding rows 'cannot be adequately trained with limited data,' so loss rises. This is the closest thing to a 'cut tokens you can't train' argument, but it is framed as count-of-tokens vs available DATA, not as a per-token firing-frequency / under-trained-token signature.
- The ONLY place a ~16K vocab appears is Figure 7 / Sec 5: a fixed 302M-param model in the DATA-CONSTRAINED regime, where empirical optimal V drops 16K->10K; with excess data it rises 16K->24K. Candidate vocab grid there was {8K,10K,16K,24K,32K,48K}. This is a 302M model, not an 8B model, and it shows the optimum MOVING with data, not a recommended cutoff.
- Scope is explicitly monolingual English, dense transformers, trained FROM SCRATCH, up to 3B validated / extrapolated to larger. Multilingual and continual-pretraining vocabulary extension are named as FUTURE WORK (Sec B.4), not covered.
- Caveat the doc should note: the paper's own related work cites Dou et al. [19] that 'expanding vocabularies during continual pre-training can lead to significant performance degradation for low-resource languages' (line 1635) — directly relevant to a Greek CPT extension and a reason for caution, not a cutoff recipe.
- Standing recommendation (line 729): use the V_opt for a given non-vocab param count assuming optimal data allocation, 'even in scenarios where overtraining may occur' — i.e. the paper leans toward the larger compute-optimal V, not a data-shrunk smaller one.

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** Tao et al. 2024 (vocabulary scaling) is the basis for choosing the ~17k Greek vocabulary cutoff (CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md line 120: 'Vocabulary-size-optimal cutoff ... the basis for the 17k cutoff').
**Verdict:** `OVERSTATED`
**Evidence:** The paper's whole thesis points the other way: 'larger models requiring larger vocabularies ... Most LLMs ... use insufficient vocabulary sizes' (Abstract, lines 22-24). Its compute-optimal predictions for the relevant scale are FAR above 17K: Table 1 gives 7B->~60-67K and 13B->~81-91K (an 8B interpolates to ~62-70K total); the smallest V_opt anywhere in Table 1 is ~37K (3B). The paper never predicts, derives, or endorses ~17K as an optimal vocabulary for any 8B-class model. The only sub-17K numbers (16K, 10K) are for a 302M data-constrained model in Figure 7. The paper also (a) is explicitly monolingual-English/from-scratch and lists multilingual + continual-pretraining vocab extension as out-of-scope future work (Sec B.4), and (b) cites Dou et al. [19] (line 1635) that vocabulary expansion during CPT can DEGRADE low-resource-language performance. So Tao supports the GENERAL PRINCIPLE that an optimal vocabulary exists and is bounded by compute/data (Sec 3, lines 305-310; Fig 3) and that adding tokens you can't train with available data hurts — which loosely motivates HAVING some cutoff — but it does not provide the value, the regime, or the per-token firing-frequency logic the doc attaches to it.
**Correction:** Reframe the citation. Tao supports the principle that vocabulary has a compute-/data-bounded optimum and that under-trainable extra tokens hurt loss (Sec 3 + Fig 3), which justifies imposing SOME cutoff rather than keeping every merged Greek token. It does NOT justify the specific ~17K value: for an 8B model Tao's compute-optimal V is roughly 60-90K total, and its only sub-17K figures are for a 302M data-constrained model (Fig 7). The 17K choice rests on the project's own fertility-knee + firing-frequency (>1,000x) analysis and on Land & Bartolo (Magikarp) for the under-trained-token signature — Tao is at most secondary/contextual support for 'an optimum exists,' and the doc should flag that Tao explicitly does not cover multilingual CPT vocab extension and cites evidence (Dou et al.) that CPT vocab expansion can hurt low-resource languages.

## How we use it
Cite Tao only for the GENERAL principle that vocabulary has a compute-/data-bounded optimum and that extra tokens which can't be adequately trained on the available data raise loss (Sec 3, lines 305-310; Fig 3) — i.e. as justification that imposing SOME Greek cutoff is sound, not as the source of the 17k value. Do NOT cite it as 'the basis for the 17k cutoff'; its compute-optimal predictions for an 8B model sit at ~60-90K total vocab and its only sub-17k numbers are for a 302M data-constrained model (Fig 7). Keep the actual 17k rationale anchored to the project's fertility-knee + firing-frequency (>1,000x) analysis and to Land & Bartolo for the under-trained-token signature. Importantly, surface the two scope caveats Tao itself raises that bear on this Greek-CPT project: (1) the paper is monolingual-English / from-scratch and names multilingual + continual-pretraining vocab extension as future work (Sec B.4), so its formulas are not directly transferable to a per-language slice of an extended tokenizer; and (2) it cites Dou et al. [19] that vocabulary expansion during CPT can significantly degrade low-resource-language performance (line 1635) — a caution worth recording in the CPT risk notes. The Table 1 figures are useful as a sanity check that the FULL extended Apertus tokenizer (~148k total) is in the right order of magnitude for an 8B model, but that is a separate point from the Greek-cutoff decision.
