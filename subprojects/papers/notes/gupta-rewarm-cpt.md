# Continual Pre-Training of Large Language Models: How to (re)warm your model? (Gupta, Thérien, Ibrahim et al., 2023, arXiv:2308.04014)

**Source:** arXiv:2308.04014  |  **Topic:** LR schedule (CPT)  |  `txt/gupta-rewarm-cpt.txt`, `pdf/gupta-rewarm-cpt.pdf`

**TL;DR.** A Pythia-410M study (Pile to SlimPajama, ~300B to ~297B tokens, val perplexity only) showing that re-warming then decaying the learning rate is necessary to learn well on new data and lets continually pre-trained models beat from-scratch training, with the max-LR height (not warmup length) as the upstream/downstream tradeoff lever — but on highly similar, overlapping datasets and explicitly not validated for language-domain shifts or larger scales.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Setup: Pythia 410M, upstream = Pile (~300B tokens), downstream = SlimPajama (~297B tokens, a deduped RedPajama built from LLaMA-style sources). Metric is validation perplexity/loss only -- no downstream task benchmarks (acknowledged as future work, lines 705-713). This is the predecessor/companion to Ibrahim et al. 2024 and shares most authors (Gupta, Thérien, Ibrahim, Richter, Anthony, Belilovsky, Rish, Lesort).
- Core finding (Takeaway 2, lines 473-476): 'Rewarming then decaying the learning rate appears necessary to learn well on the downstream task.' A constant (un-rewarmed) low LR is initially advantageous on the upstream set but that advantage vanishes once trained long enough downstream.
- Two-sided result on re-warming (Takeaway 3, lines 609-623): re-warming is ALSO 'a significant cause for the degradation of performance' early in downstream training -- the same warmup-induced loss spike appears even when fine-tuning on the SAME data (Pile->Pile, Fig. 5), and models 'do not appear to be able to recover from the performance hit' in the same-data case. So re-warming is a necessary-but-costly transient; the doc must not present it as free.
- Warmup LENGTH barely matters; warmup HEIGHT (max LR) is the real lever. Tested warmup lengths 0% / 0.5% / 1% / 2% of 297B tokens -> 'does not appear to have a significant effect' (Takeaway 1, lines 314-316). Progressive warmup is 'not necessary' but skipping it creates a 'chaotic phase' / stability-gap loss spike with 'no consequences later' (lines 160-162, 342-346).
- Max-LR height trades off forgetting vs adaptation (Takeaway 2 / Fig. 2,3,4): higher max LR -> better downstream (SlimPajama) but more upstream (Pile) forgetting; lower max LR -> preserves upstream, weaker downstream adaptation. Concrete LRs used: {1.5e-4, 3e-4, 6e-4}, with Pythia-410M default 3e-4 (lines 174-176, 348-353).
- Beats from-scratch: at convergence, all warmed-up fine-tuned models outperform a model trained from scratch on the combined data, even though the downstream set is on the scale of the upstream set (lines 504-510, 518-519, 579-584, Conclusion lines 723-728). Caveat: a constant-LR model and an early-stopped model can be more economical within the first ~100-200B tokens.
- Schedule details: AdamW (beta1=0.9, beta2=0.95, eps=1e-8, weight decay 0.1), cosine decay to MinLr = 0.1 * MaxLr, grad clip 1.0, FP16, no dropout, no replay. Cosine reaches min LR at 240B tokens then constant. Latest checkpoint is best to re-warm from; earlier checkpoints do NOT speed downstream learning, and pre-training did not cause loss of plasticity (Takeaways 3-4, lines 167, 715-720, 750-752).
- Critical generalization caveat for a Greek project: upstream and downstream here are highly similar with explicit data overlap ('positive transfer', 'synergy'). The Discussion (lines 758-764) states results 'may not generalize to setups with different distribution shifts, such as language domain' -- exactly the regime of English-base -> Greek CPT. Treat the rewarm prescription as directionally supportive, not quantitatively transferable.

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** Re-warming the LR is needed/beneficial for continual pretraining (companion evidence to Ibrahim).
**Verdict:** `PARTIAL`
**Evidence:** Abstract (lines 35-50): hypothesis is 'the learning rate must be re-increased to improve compute efficiency when training on a new dataset'; results show 'while rewarming models first increases the loss on upstream and downstream data, in the longer run it improves the downstream performance, outperforming models trained from scratch.' Takeaway 2 (lines 473-476): 'Rewarming then decaying the learning rate appears necessary to learn well on the downstream task.' Conclusion (lines 723-728): models that are rewarmed improve over models trained from scratch, motivating continual pre-training. BUT Takeaway 3 (lines 609-623) shows re-warming is ALSO 'a significant cause for the degradation of performance' early on, and in the same-data setting models 'do not appear to be able to recover from the performance hit.' Discussion (lines 758-764) restricts generality: 'our results may not generalize to setups with different distribution shifts, such as language domain.'
**Correction:** Keep the citation but add the conditions the doc omits: (1) it is re-warming THEN cosine-decaying (not re-warming alone) that is 'necessary to learn well downstream' -- the benefit is at convergence, not early; re-warming causes a transient loss spike on both upstream and downstream data. (2) The lever proven beneficial is the max-LR HEIGHT (forgetting/adaptation tradeoff), while warmup LENGTH was found NOT to matter and progressive warmup was deemed 'not necessary' (only avoids an inconsequential stability-gap spike). (3) Evidence is Pythia-410M, perplexity-only, on highly similar overlapping English datasets (Pile->SlimPajama); the authors explicitly say it may not transfer to language-domain shifts -- i.e. it is suggestive, not proof, for English->Greek CPT. Phrase as 'companion/predecessor evidence to Ibrahim et al. 2024' rather than independent confirmation, since it shares most authors and the same lab/lineage.

## How we use it
Use this as the originating, small-scale (410M, perplexity-only) motivation for re-warming the LR in Apertus-8B Greek CPT, paired with Ibrahim et al. 2024 (which scales the same finding to billions of params, adds replay, and tests real distribution/language shifts). Concretely: (1) Cite it to justify re-increasing the LR above the base model's final/min LR and then cosine-decaying, rather than fine-tuning at a vanishing constant LR. (2) Adopt its framing that the max-LR HEIGHT is the forgetting-vs-adaptation knob -- a higher rewarm LR buys stronger Greek adaptation at the cost of more English forgetting -- and treat warmup LENGTH as a low-sensitivity hyperparameter (a short 0.5-1% warmup mainly to dodge the stability-gap spike, not to be tuned for performance). (3) Do NOT port the specific numbers (1.5e-4 / 3e-4 / 6e-4, MinLr=0.1*MaxLr, decay-to-min-at-240B) as targets -- they are Pythia-410M scale and base-LR specific; derive our rewarm peak relative to Apertus-8B's own pretraining LR. (4) Flag the load-bearing caveat in CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md: this paper's upstream/downstream are near-identical overlapping English corpora, and the authors explicitly disclaim language-domain shift, which is precisely our regime -- so expect a larger forgetting penalty and a more important role for replay (covered by Ibrahim, not here) than this paper's optimistic from-scratch comparison implies. Anchor the rewarm-then-decay schedule choice to Ibrahim for the quantitative recipe and to Gupta for the mechanism/intuition.
