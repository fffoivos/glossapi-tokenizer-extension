# A Practitioner's Guide to Continual Multimodal Pretraining (FoMo-in-Flux)

**Source:** arXiv:2408.14471  |  **Topic:** LR schedule (CPT)  |  `txt/practitioner-multimodal-cpt.txt`, `pdf/practitioner-multimodal-cpt.pdf`

**TL;DR.** A large-scale empirical "practitioner's guide" to continual multimodal (CLIP/OpenCLIP) pretraining, introducing the FoMo-in-Flux benchmark (63 datasets) and sweeping data mixtures, update methods, learning-rate / meta-schedules, and model/compute scaling to characterize the knowledge-accumulation vs. zero-shot-retention trade-off.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Domain caveat (load-bearing): this is a CONTRASTIVE VISION-LANGUAGE (OpenCLIP) study, not a decoder-only text-LLM study. All LR numbers, metrics, and trade-off curves are for CLIP continual pretraining on image-text data. Transfer to text-only Apertus-8B CPT is by analogy, not direct evidence.
- Two axes of merit are defined as metrics (Sec. 5, l.1593-1597): Knowledge Accumulation A_KA = avg accuracy/recall@5 over 41 adaptation datasets; Zero-Shot Retention A_ZS = zero-shot transfer accuracy/recall@5 over 22 held-out datasets. Plots live on the A_KA-A_ZS plane.
- Fig. 9 sweeps base LR over {1e-4, 1e-5, 1e-6, 1e-7} for full-FT, each with a joint-training upper bound, and overlays contour lines of the geometric mean sqrt(A_KA x A_ZS). This is the explicit adaptation-vs-retention curve over base LR.
- The 1e-5 reference is DERIVED from the original OpenCLIP pretraining peak LR in Cherti et al. (1e-3), then corrected for a smaller batch size (b_ours=512 vs b_openclip=88064). Linear resizing gives ~5.81e-6, sqrt resizing gives ~7.625e-5; they rounded the linear-resized value UP to 1e-5 as a 'cleaner entry point' that 'worked slightly better' in preliminary experiments (l.2052-2078).
- Finding (l.2074-2078): 1e-5 has 'the highest degree of knowledge accumulation' AND 'the highest base joint tradeoff with respect to zero-shot retention'; 'Larger learning rates incur significantly higher rates of particularly early-task forgetting, while smaller learning rates limit the amount of knowledge gained.' Fig. 9 caption: 1e-5 'achieves the highest final knowledge accumulation and provides the optimal balance between A_KA and A_ZS.'
- LR-schedule shape matters less than meta-schedule: switching the per-task scheduler (cosine vs rsqrt) yields 'negligible change' in A_KA and retention over a 20-task cycle (l.2171-2175); 'matching the original pretraining scheduler is of lesser importance' (also abstract insight l.169-172).
- The paper's actual headline LR contribution is META learning-rate schedules (task-level schedules over per-task LR schedules, e.g. autoregressive cosine), which 'can significantly break forgetting while allowing for nearly the same degree of knowledge accumulation' and are hyperparameter-free (l.1988-1993).
- Other practitioner findings: larger model size reduces the accumulation-vs-retention trade-off (and can give positive backward transfer); compute scaling only helps the trade-off when combined with model merging; full-model tuning beats locked-encoder training over long cycles; an initial stability gap is mitigated by matching pretraining/CPT softmax temperatures (calibration); replaying previous ADAPTATION tasks helps more than replaying pretraining data (l.1994-2005, 181-184).

## How we use it
Use as a directional CPT prior, not a source of transferable numbers, because it studies OpenCLIP (contrastive vision-language) continual pretraining, whereas our project is text-only continued pretraining of Apertus-8B. Two principles port cleanly to our Greek CPT: (1) derive the CPT base/peak LR from the model's ORIGINAL pretraining peak LR rather than picking a fresh large value, and batch-size-rescale it (linear or sqrt) -- for Apertus we should look up its actual pretraining peak LR and rescale to our batch size rather than borrowing the paper's 1e-5, which is a CLIP-specific b=512 number with no authority for an 8B decoder. (2) Expect larger LRs to cause disproportionately more early-step forgetting of base (here, English/multilingual) capabilities and smaller LRs to under-learn the new Greek distribution -- i.e., the LR knob trades adaptation against retention, so it should be tuned on a small adaptation-vs-retention sweep before the full run. Secondary takeaways worth carrying: per-task LR-schedule shape (cosine vs rsqrt) matters little, warmup to the pretraining peak is standard; replaying base/pretraining data is less critical than the method literature assumes (relevant to our Greek/base mix ratio); and meta-schedules / model-merging are the paper's stronger forgetting mitigations if we ever do multi-stage updates. Cite it in CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md only for the qualitative 'reuse-rescaled-pretraining-LR, larger-LR-is-more-forgetting-prone' claim, with an explicit note that the 1e-5 figure is not directly applicable.
