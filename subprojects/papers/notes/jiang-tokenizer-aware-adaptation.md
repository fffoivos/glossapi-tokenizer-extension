# Tokenizer-Aware Cross-Lingual Adaptation of Decoder-Only LLMs through Embedding Relearning and Swapping

**Source:** ACL 2026.eacl-long.357  |  **Topic:** New-token init  |  `txt/jiang-tokenizer-aware-adaptation.txt`, `pdf/jiang-tokenizer-aware-adaptation.pdf`

**TL;DR.** Extends Artetxe-style embedding relearning (swap tokenizer, retrain only the embedding layer on a frozen transformer body) from old encoder-only models to modern decoder-only LLMs (Gemma2 2B/9B/27B, Aya23, PaLM2) across 96+ languages, showing it is light-weight, competitive with full-weight CPT, and mitigates catastrophic forgetting — with a generation-task weakness that is fixed by frozen-body LoRA, not by unfreezing.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Method (EmbSwap): two parallel cheap stages on the SAME base LLM — (1) Language-Adapt: swap in a custom tokenizer and tune ONLY the new embedding layer on 200B multilingual tokens with the transformer body frozen; (2) English instruction-tune (FLAN, 20B tokens) the body with original embeddings frozen. Then combine the multilingual embeddings with the IT body. Body stays frozen throughout (lines 327-340).
- Scale and models: Gemma2 2B/9B/27B are the primary testbed; also Aya23 (8B/35B) and PaLM2 (XXS/S). 212 SEA + 392 AFR + 170 IND languages; evaluation across 96 diverse languages on classification + generation. This is a genuine at-scale decoder-only result, not a toy study (lines 378-392).
- Headline numbers: embedding relearning improves Gemma2 by up to 20% (abstract); EmbSwap gives up to ~10% gains on classification tasks; only embeddings tuned = 3x22.5% of params per region vs 100% for full CPT (Table 1, line 648). Training is cheap: embedding tuning converges within ~24h; LoRA-Adaptation within ~12h even for 27B (lines 2025-2032).
- Forgetting result: 'EmbSwap mitigates catastrophic forgetting' (line 1286). Full-weight CPT and ChatVector boost transfer but suffer large English/multilingual drops; EmbSwap shows 'only minor regressions in English' because it reuses (frozen) existing knowledge, and notably preserves multilingual math reasoning vs full-weight updating (lines 1286-1295).
- Generation-quality caveat (LOAD-BEARING for this project): the auto-regressive generation paradigm makes the model sensitive to embedding changes (error propagation over time steps), so pure EmbSwap 'sometimes underperforms an instruction-tuned baseline on generative tasks' / 'often leading to performance degradation' (lines 344-346, 1174-1181). The embedding layer is used for both encoding and decoding, which classification (small solution space) tolerates but generation does not.
- Fix for the generation caveat is LoRA on a FROZEN body, NOT unfreezing: insert LoRA (rank 64) into the self-attention of all transformer layers and 'exclusively optimize these weights' while 'keeping both the body and embeddings frozen', tuned on a small 4B-token Dmix (10% IT + equal LA). Gives +5.4% avg on generation for 27B (lines 348-351, 1182-1184, 2027-2028).
- Paper explicitly warns AGAINST full-parameter tuning: it 'could exacerbate the problem of catastrophic forgetting, while embedding tuning provides a safer alternative' (lines 3189-3194). Full-parameter CPT is the costly baseline they beat on forgetting, not a recommendation.
- Recipe caveats: initialization of new embeddings from original subtoken embeddings is crucial (averaging vs max-pooling barely matters, line 362); custom tokenizers help most for non-Latin scripts; a multilingual warm-up is necessary before embedding relearning if the base LLM has weak multilingual ability (Gemma-2B fails without it, lines 3158-3166); residual slight English/HRL regression persists, attributed to lower-quality HRL data in the adaptation corpus (lines 1549-1559).

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** Embedding relearning on decoder-only LLMs at scale; mitigates forgetting.
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** Title: 'Tokenizer-Aware Cross-Lingual Adaptation of Decoder-Only LLMs through Embedding Relearning and Swapping.' Abstract (lines 17-26): prior relearning 'has only been shown to work for older generation encoder-only models ... In this paper, we extend this framework to decoder-only LLMs ... we show embedding relearning improves Gemma2 models by up to 20% ... mitigating catastrophic forgetting.' Scale is real: Gemma2 2B/9B/27B + Aya23 + PaLM2, 200B-token language-adaptation corpus (line 327), 96 languages (line 22). Results section: 'EmbSwap mitigates catastrophic forgetting ... EmbSwap shows only minor regressions in English' (lines 1286-1291).

**Claim:** A generation-quality caveat argues for unfreezing the body rather than staying frozen.
**Verdict:** `CONTRADICTED`
**Evidence:** The generation-quality caveat exists but the paper's response is the OPPOSITE of unfreezing. Lines 344-346: EmbSwap 'is effective for discriminative tasks but sometimes underperforms an instruction-tuned baseline on generative tasks.' Lines 1174-1181 explain the auto-regressive sensitivity. The remedy (lines 348-351): 'we insert LoRA weights into the self-attention layer ... These weights are then fine-tuned ... while KEEPING BOTH THE BODY AND EMBEDDINGS FROZEN.' Line 2027-2028: LoRA 'exclusively optimize these weights.' The body is never unfrozen. Conversely, the paper warns full-parameter tuning 'could exacerbate the problem of catastrophic forgetting, while embedding tuning provides a safer alternative' (lines 3189-3194), and full-weight CPT is the forgetting-prone baseline they beat (lines 1286-1295).
**Correction:** Replace the claim. Correct framing: the generation-quality caveat (pure embedding-swap degrades generative tasks due to encode/decode coupling and auto-regressive error propagation) is solved by adding FROZEN-body LoRA adapters to self-attention (rank 64, ~4B tokens, +5.4% gen on 27B), NOT by unfreezing the transformer body. The paper argues the opposite of this claim: keep the body frozen and route the small generation fix through low-rank adapters, precisely to avoid the catastrophic forgetting that full-parameter / unfrozen-body tuning causes. If the config doc wants a paper-backed argument to unfreeze/train the body, THIS paper does not provide it — it provides the argument to keep it frozen.

## How we use it
Use this paper as the methodological anchor for the cheap end of the Greek-adaptation design space, but do NOT cite it to justify unfreezing the Apertus body. It validates that you can swap to a Greek-extended tokenizer and retrain only the embedding layer (with new embeddings initialized from original subtoken embeddings — directly relevant since our merge-rule extension preserves first-1000 ids and adds new tokens) on a frozen body and get strong, forgetting-safe cross-lingual transfer at up-to-27B scale; the 'multilingual warm-up is necessary if base multilinguality is weak' caveat is reassuring because Apertus-8B already has Greek exposure, but worth checking. The load-bearing transfer to our project: if/when we observe Greek GENERATION quality lagging after an embedding-only CPT pass, the paper's evidence-backed fix is frozen-body LoRA on self-attention (rank 64, small mixed corpus), NOT a full-body unfreeze — and the paper explicitly flags full-parameter tuning as the catastrophic-forgetting risk we are trying to avoid.
