# Efficient Continual Pre-training by Mitigating the Stability Gap (arXiv:2406.14833)

**Source:** arXiv:2406.14833  |  **Topic:** LR schedule (CPT)  |  `txt/stability-gap-cpt.txt`, `pdf/stability-gap-cpt.pdf`

**TL;DR.** During continual pre-training, an LLM's target-domain AND general task performance first drops then recovers (a V-shape "stability gap"); training on a small high-quality subset for multiple epochs plus a pre-training-matched data mixture mitigates the gap, recovering general ability and improving domain performance at a fraction of the compute budget.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Stability gap is real for LLM CPT and affects GENERAL capability, not just the target domain: general/common-sense task performance follows the same V-shape (drop then recover) as domain tasks. Verified in two settings: medical CPT (Fig 3a, OpenLLaMa) and general CPT (Fig 2c, Pythia-410m on RefinedWeb), and across scales (TinyLlama-1.1B, OpenLlama-3B, Pythia-410m). Conclusion: 'a significant initial performance drop followed by a slow recovery.'
- Mechanistic framing (their hypothesis, not proof): decompose the gradient L = α·L_plasticity + (1−α)·L_stability. Early in CPT the plasticity gradient (learn new domain) exceeds the stability gradient (preserve instruction-following), so general ability degrades first; the stability gradient then rises and performance recovers. Weight-level evidence: bottom 5 layers update more than top 5 early (rate > 1.35), consistent with top layers under-protecting high-level task knowledge initially.
- Three mitigation strategies. (I) Train on a properly-sized RANDOM subset for multiple epochs instead of the full corpus once — faster recovery; peak at ~4th epoch. (II) Pick the HIGHEST-QUALITY subset (lowest KenLM perplexity vs a domain reference) — faster + stronger recovery. (III) Use a data MIXTURE RATE matching the pre-training data to reduce distribution shift and stabilize instruction-following.
- Distribution-MATCHED replay beats plain replay. Strategy III replaces ~82% of a Llama-mixture-rate 5B-token set (the CC/C4 portion) with high-quality medical tokens, keeping the rest as pre-training-like data. Table 1: following the pre-training mixture rate for replay is 'more effective than randomly sampling pre-training data for replay' because it further reduces distribution shift. 'rate-fixed-data-dynamic' (resample replay tokens each epoch) achieves higher peak than 'rate-fixed-data-fixed'.
- Plain replay alone is a WEAK baseline in their results. Table 1 (OpenLLaMa-3B, zero-shot avg over MMLU-Med/PubMedQA/MedMCQA/MedQA-4): base 36.2; full-token 1-epoch 50B baseline 37.4; LR re-warm/re-decay 37.7; 'Replay 10% data' (random RefinedWeb) 37.5; their full strategies 40.7 at only 20B tokens; replay-10%+their-strategies 38.5 at 20B. So generic replay barely moves the needle; the subset+quality+mixture-rate combination is what drives the gain.
- Headline efficiency result: OpenLlama-3B medical avg 36.2% → 40.7% (+4.5) using only 20B tokens (40% of the 50B full-token budget). Larger application: Llama-3-8B → Llama-3-Physician-8B, best medical performance among open-source peers, comparable to/better than GPT-4 on several medical benchmarks.
- Hyperparameter caveats. Optimal LR is scale-dependent: TinyLlama-1.1B wants higher LR (3e-4), OpenLlama-3B wants lower (3e-5); too-high LR inflates the plasticity gradient and destroys instruction-following. Llama-3-8B CPT: 16×H100, AdamW (β1=0.9, β2=0.95, wd=0.01), LR 3e-5, cosine schedule with 0.1 warmup ratio, ctx 8192, bf16.
- Limitations stated by the authors: (a) ideal mixture matching requires knowing the base model's pre-training mixture, which most open models don't disclose (their Llama-3-8B run worked anyway, approximating it); (b) Strategy II's quality filter did NOT transfer to the GENERAL CPT setting — a KenLM-on-WikiText quality filter 'does not work' for general ability, so only Strategies I and III were shown to help general CPT (Fig 8); (c) largest model tested is 8B — they explicitly defer verification on larger LLMs.
- Compute budgets used: 50B tokens for the medical OpenLlama study; 100B-token budget cap for the general Pythia-410m study (best general result at 11B tokens × 7 epochs).

**Claim:** There is a temporary general-capability dip (stability gap) under CPT that replay and distribution-matching mitigate.

## How we use it
Use this as the primary citation for why our Apertus-8B Greek CPT must include a replay / distribution-matching component, and to set concrete expectations. Three concrete take-aways for our recipe: (1) Expect a V-shaped dip in GENERAL (English / multitask) capability early in Greek CPT and budget eval checkpoints to catch the recovery, not just the trough — a single early eval will look like catastrophic forgetting when it's the stability gap. (2) Do NOT rely on plain random replay: the paper shows generic 10% replay is nearly flat; what works is matching the replay mixture to Apertus's pre-training distribution. Since Apertus's exact pre-training mixture is partially documented, approximate it (the paper's Llama-3-8B run succeeded by approximating an unknown mixture) and prefer resampling replay tokens each epoch ('rate-fixed-data-dynamic' beat the fixed variant). (3) Favor a properly-sized high-quality Greek subset trained for a few epochs (peak ~epoch 4 in their medical run) over a single pass on the full corpus — this both mitigates the gap and saves compute (they hit +4.5 medical at 40% budget). Caveats to carry forward: their quality filter did NOT transfer to general-capability CPT, so don't assume our GlossAPI quality ranking will protect English/general ability — the mixture-rate (Strategy III) is the lever that protects general ability, the quality filter (Strategy II) is the lever that boosts the target domain. LR is scale-sensitive; their 8B CPT used 3e-5 cosine with 0.1 warmup on 16×H100, a reasonable starting anchor. Note the evidence is ≤8B and not cross-lingual in their own experiments, so treat the Greek (cross-lingual, larger distribution shift) case as plausibly worse — i.e., a stronger argument FOR replay, not a guarantee the same budgets suffice.
