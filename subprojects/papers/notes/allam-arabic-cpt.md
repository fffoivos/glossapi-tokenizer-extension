# ALLaM: Large Language Models for Arabic and English

**Source:** arXiv:2407.15390  |  **Topic:** Embedding LR  |  `txt/allam-arabic-cpt.txt`, `pdf/allam-arabic-cpt.pdf`

**TL;DR.** ALLaM adapts Llama-2 (7B/13B/70B) into a strong Arabic+English model via tokenizer/vocabulary expansion plus continued pretraining on 1.2T tokens, achieving SOTA Arabic results without catastrophic forgetting in English; the new-token embeddings are initialized from averaged existing embeddings and trained with a single uniform learning rate.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Vocabulary-extension recipe is two steps: (i) tokenizer augmentation (train a target-language tokenizer, then MERGE it into the original by adding only tokens not already present), and (ii) expanded vocabulary learning (lines 666-675).
- New-token embedding initialization: tokenize each new token with the ORIGINAL tokenizer, average the original embeddings of those pieces, and assign that as the new token's vector (byte-fallback guarantees a tokenization exists). This 'gives a significant boost' and 'speeds up learning dramatically' vs random init (lines 676-683, Figure 6 lines 727-729).
- CPT learning rate is UNIFORM across the whole model: they 'used the final learning rate of the pretrained language model (usually 3e-5)' for all continued-pretraining experiments (lines 687-688). No per-row or per-parameter-group LR is mentioned anywhere in the paper.
- They TRIED varying the LR ('gradually increase and decay the learning rate') and it failed: 'with limited success, as such models typically exhibited catastrophic forgetting' (lines 688-689). They also tried optimizer-state warmup, with 'little effect' (lines 689-691). So schedule/LR manipulation was actively rejected, not just unused.
- Scale of the CPT run: continue-pretrain ALLaM-7B/13B on 1.2T tokens (English+Arabic), ALLaM-70B on 600B tokens, same data mixture, all from Llama-2 weights (lines 684-686).
- Dropout ablation (Figure 7): adding 10% dropout marginally helps Arabic acquisition but hurts source-language (English) performance (Llama-2 was pretrained without dropout on 2T tokens); they chose NO dropout for CPT (lines 691-695, 754-757).
- From-scratch recipe (no vocab expansion needed): 4T English tokens then 1.2T mixed Arabic/English; matched Llama-2 hyperparameters incl. 4M tokens/batch and max LR 3e-4 decayed to 3e-5 with a cosine schedule (lines 767-771). Note this cosine schedule applies to from-scratch, NOT to the vocab-extension CPT, which uses the flat 3e-5.
- Related-work context: notes alternatives like Artetxe et al. (2019) transferring a monolingual model by 'learning only the word embeddings in the target language' (lines 1874-1876) — a freeze-body/train-embeddings approach that ALLaM does NOT adopt; ALLaM trains the full model.

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** Documented practice for vocabulary-extension CPT is uniform LR + good init (not a differential/higher new-row LR).
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** Uniform LR: 'In all of our continued pretraining experiments, we used the final learning rate of the pretrained language model (usually 3 x 10-5)' (lines 687-688). They actively rejected schedule manipulation: 'We experimented with approaches to gradually increase and decay the learning rate with limited success, as such models typically exhibited catastrophic forgetting' (lines 688-689). Good init: new-token embeddings are initialized by averaging the original-tokenizer embeddings of the token's pieces, and 'Initializing the new embeddings from the combination of previously learned embeddings gives a significant boost to the learning of a new language' (lines 679-683); Figure 6: 'initializing the embeddings for new tokens from combinations of existing embeddings speeds up learning dramatically' (lines 727-729). A targeted grep for any differential / new-row / per-parameter-group / higher-LR-for-embeddings language returned nothing — the paper never proposes giving new rows a separate or higher LR.

## How we use it
Anchor citation for the embedding-adaptation + CPT LR decision in subprojects/03_apertus_extension_and_embedding_adaptation. It supports two design choices for the Apertus-8B Greek extension: (1) initialize the new Greek merge-rule rows from averaged Apertus embeddings of each new token's existing-tokenizer pieces (mean-of-pieces init), which ALLaM shows dramatically speeds learning vs random; and (2) run CPT with a single flat LR pinned near the base model's final/min LR rather than a warmup-then-decay schedule, since ALLaM found schedule manipulation drove catastrophic forgetting of the source language. Caveat to carry into CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md: ALLaM is a positive existence proof for 'uniform LR + good init', not a controlled rejection of a differential/higher new-row LR — they never ran that experiment, so cite it as 'precedent for uniform LR working well,' not as 'evidence that differential LR is worse.' Two scale/architecture differences to flag: ALLaM trained on 1.2T tokens (far beyond our budget) and used tied-vs-untied embeddings unstated; our project has tie_word_embeddings=false and vocab 156672, so the new rows are independently learnable output+input embeddings. Also do not import ALLaM's 3e-4->3e-5 cosine as a CPT schedule — that is their from-scratch recipe; the vocab-extension CPT used the flat ~3e-5.
