# On the Adequacy of Untuned Warmup for Adaptive Optimization (Ma & Yarats, arXiv:1910.04209)

**Source:** arXiv:1910.04209  |  **Topic:** Warmup  |  `txt/ma-yarats-warmup.txt`, `pdf/ma-yarats-warmup.pdf`

**TL;DR.** Refutes RAdam's variance-based motivation and shows that simple untuned linear warmup of Adam over 2/(1-beta2) iterations matches RAdam across settings, motivating warmup instead by Adam's oversized early update-step magnitudes (which persist even at a converged minimum).

## Key load-bearing facts
- Recommended untuned default: linear warmup of Adam's learning rate over tau = 2*(1-beta2)^-1 iterations (Abstract; lines 101-103, 489-498, 911-914). For beta2=0.999 this is 2000 iterations.
- The second-moment-reliability intuition justifies the EXPONENTIAL rule tau=(1-beta2)^-1, not the linear 2/(1-beta2) value. The exponential warmup factor is set ~equal to Adam's bias-correction term 1-beta2^t, interpreted as 'how complete the second moment estimator is' (lines 452-476). The authors call this intuition 'extremely speculative' (line 457).
- The linear rule's factor of 2 is NOT a second-moment argument: it multiplies the exponential tau by 2 to 'preserve the same effective warmup period' because linear warmup does not decelerate the way exponential does (lines 499-503, Appendix B.2).
- Core mechanism (Section 3.3): even initialized at an idealized local minimum (i.i.d. zero-mean gradients), Adam's update-step magnitudes start at 1*alpha and only decay to a stationary distribution (~0.15*alpha) after ~40 iterations (lines 369-401, Figure 2). SGD restarted from a near-converged model needs no warmup; Adam does (lines 365-401). This is the load-bearing 'restart from a minimum still needs warmup' fact.
- The rule is anchored empirically by RAdam-equivalence: RAdam and the rule-of-thumb schedules have nearly identical effective warmup periods across practical beta2, and RAdam is shown to be ~4 steps of momentum SGD followed by Adam with a fixed warmup schedule (lines 509-525, 900-902).
- Important caveat the rule does NOT capture: update magnitudes settle in ~40-100 iterations, and the rule-of-thumb effective warmup is ~1000 iterations for beta2=0.999, but real SOTA NLP recipes use up to 10000 linear-warmup iterations; the authors admit the precise channel requiring extended warmup is 'still an unresolved question' (lines 853-867). So 2/(1-beta2) is a floor/starting point, not a tuned optimum for large LM training.
- Update-step magnitudes in later training are largely invariant to gradient distribution (Section 6.2, lines 874-876) -- a reason adaptive-LR variance is a poor lens for warmup.

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** Adam-family optimizers take oversized early steps even when restarted at a minimum (why a re-warm is needed even from a converged checkpoint).
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** Section 3.3 simulates gradients as i.i.d. zero-mean normal 'thus approximating the optimization dynamics at an exact local minimum' (lines 375-383); Figure 2 + text: 'the update magnitudes for all parameters start at 1 * alpha ... only beginning to settle into a stationary distribution after 40 or so training iterations (with median update magnitude ~0.16*alpha)' (lines 384-396). Conclusion: 'unlike SGD, Adam will always encounter early-stage training instability by way of large update magnitudes, even when the model is already initialized at a local minimum' (lines 397-401). Explicit SGD contrast: 'a nearly-converged model ... trained via SGD can have its optimization be stably restarted without learning rate warmup. This is not the case with Adam.' (lines 367-369). Conclusion restates it: warmup need is 'partially explained by inspecting Adam's early-stage update step magnitudes when applied to an already-converged model' (lines 904-906).

**Claim:** The 2/(1-beta2) value is the untuned-warmup horizon over which the second-moment estimate becomes reliable (this paper is the source of that rule).
**Verdict:** `OVERSTATED`
**Evidence:** The paper IS the source of the linear 2*(1-beta2)^-1 rule (lines 101-103, 489-498, 911-914), so the provenance part is correct. But the 'horizon over which the second-moment estimate becomes reliable' justification applies to the EXPONENTIAL rule tau=(1-beta2)^-1, not to the 2/(1-beta2) value. Lines 467-471: the exponential warmup factor is set ~equal to the bias-correction term 1-beta2^t, which 'is the sum of the coefficients in the moving average estimation of the second moment, and can thus be interpreted as how complete the second moment estimator is at any given point in time.' The authors call this 'our guiding (albeit extremely speculative) intuition' (line 457). The factor of 2 in the LINEAR rule is explicitly NOT a second-moment argument: 'the tau from the exponential rule-of-thumb is multiplied by 2 to account for the fact that exponential warmup decelerates over time, whereas linear warmup does not' to 'preserve the same effective warmup period' (lines 499-503). The rule is also anchored by RAdam-equivalence, not a reliability-horizon derivation (lines 509-525).
**Correction:** Reword to: 'Ma & Yarats (2019) recommend, as a speculative rule of thumb, untuned LINEAR warmup over 2/(1-beta2) iterations. The (1-beta2)^-1 base horizon is loosely motivated by Adam's second-moment bias-correction term 1-beta2^t -- a proxy for how complete the second-moment estimate is -- but the authors call this intuition extremely speculative, and the factor of 2 is added purely to match the effective warmup period of their exponential rule, not for any second-moment-reliability reason. The rule is validated empirically via equivalence to RAdam, not derived as a reliability horizon.' Do not state that 2/(1-beta2) is THE horizon at which the second moment becomes reliable; that conflates the linear and exponential rules and upgrades a self-described speculative heuristic to a theoretical result.
