# Scaling Laws and Compute-Optimal Training Beyond Fixed Training Durations (Hägele et al., 2024; arXiv:2405.18392)

**Source:** arXiv:2405.18392  |  **Topic:** LR schedule  |  `txt/hagele-wsd-scaling.txt`, `pdf/hagele-wsd-scaling.pdf`

**TL;DR.** EPFL/HuggingFace study showing that a constant-LR-plus-cooldown schedule (the trapezoidal / WSD recipe) matches a well-tuned cosine schedule, scales predictably, and enables cheap scaling-law research and continual training from pre-cooldown checkpoints.

## Key load-bearing facts
- The paper does NOT invent WSD; it ANALYZES it. It explicitly attributes the schedule to prior work: 'trapezoidal (Zhai et al., 2022), and later as the warmup-stable-decay schedule (WSD) (Hu et al., 2024)' (lines 123-125), and deliberately renames it 'constant LR + cooldown' to avoid term overloading (line 127).
- Central result: a constant LR for the majority of training plus a short final cooldown 'matches the performance' of a well-tuned cosine schedule, with slightly LESS sensitivity to the max-LR choice (Takeaway 1, line 348; Fig. 3, line 282-283).
- Cooldown length: benefits plateau around 20% of total steps; they use 20% for most experiments. For long runs even <20% (e.g. ~5%, or 10k of 200k steps) nearly matches cosine, because what matters is enough cooldown in ABSOLUTE steps (lines 352-358, 462-469; Takeaways 3).
- Cooldown SHAPE matters: they introduce a (1-sqrt) decay form, f(n,N,Ndecay)=1-sqrt((n-(N-Ndecay))/Ndecay), that consistently beats linear decay, more so for longer runs (Takeaway 2, lines 340-351; sqrt exponent a=0.5 is best, lines 1742-1919).
- Cooldown typically decays the LR to (near) zero, mirroring warmup, giving a trapezoidal shape (Eq. 1, lines 164-189). Note: annealing all the way to zero can HURT downstream benchmark metrics (early saturation), so max and final LR 'should be set (and ideally swept over) independently' (lines 482-489).
- The 'half' statements (the two places this could be cited): (a) line 283 - in their LR-sensitivity sweep, the cooldown's optimum 'lies slightly below at half of the optimal cosine maximum LR' (empirical observation); (b) line 840 - in the scaling-law experiments they 'set the constant LR to be half the maximum LR for cosine' for a FAIR COMPARISON, paired with 20% linear cooldown. Both refer to the CONSTANT/PLATEAU LR vs cosine's PEAK, not to a generic 'WSD peak = 0.5x cosine peak' guideline.
- Continual learning is a built-in advantage: continue training from a checkpoint BEFORE the cooldown at the high constant LR, which avoids the loss spikes / brittleness of re-warming a decayed cosine model (lines 198-204). Re-warming is reported to hurt and cause forgetting (Ibrahim et al., 2024), though careful strategies can alleviate it.
- Data-mixture / curriculum changes can be applied DURING the cooldown as a form of finetuning (Hu et al., 2024; Llama 3 example) - relevant if a Greek-heavy mix is upweighted in the decay phase (lines 205-209). The paper itself keeps the mixture fixed and leaves curriculum questions open.
- Scale of evidence (caveat for an 8B project): experiments are SMALL - models 33M-360M (some up to 360M) on SlimPajama, token scales 0.3B-10B around Chinchilla D/N~20, batch 0.5M, 300 warmup steps. No 8B-scale or continued-pretraining-of-an-existing-model validation. Findings are claimed to scale 'reliably similar to cosine' but were established in this small regime.

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** This paper is the source of the WSD scheduler analysis.
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** The paper is squarely an analysis of the constant-LR+cooldown (= trapezoidal = WSD) schedule and its scaling behavior. Abstract (line 19-20): 'we investigate the training behavior of a direct alternative -- constant learning rate and cooldowns -- and find that it scales predictably and reliably similar to cosine.' Lines 123-127 name the schedule: 'referred to as a trapezoidal (Zhai et al., 2022), and later as the warmup-stable-decay schedule (WSD) (Hu et al., 2024). To avoid overloading of terms ... we refer to this approach as constant LR + cooldown.' Eq. 1 (lines 164-189) and Takeaways 1-4 (lines 348-481) constitute the analysis.

**Claim:** Provides the guideline to set max LR to roughly half the equivalent cosine value -- the origin of the project 'half' framing (peak = 0.5x Apertus).
**Verdict:** `OVERSTATED`
**Evidence:** The paper never states a prescriptive guideline that one's WSD/cooldown PEAK LR should be set to ~half the cosine peak. Two related-but-weaker statements exist. (1) Line 282-283 (Fig. 3 caption) is a descriptive empirical observation from one LR sweep: 'We find the LR sensitivity (right) to be similar for both schedules, albeit less for cooldown, where the optimum lies slightly below at half of the optimal cosine maximum LR.' (2) Line 839-841 is a methodological choice for comparability, not advice: 'In line with the findings of Sect. 3 and for a fair comparison, we set the constant LR to be half the maximum LR for cosine and perform 20% (linear) cooldown steps.' In (2) 'the constant LR' is the plateau LR of the constant+cooldown run set to half of cosine's PEAK -- the schedule cools down FROM that constant value toward zero, so the cooldown peak equals the constant LR.
**Correction:** Soften the doc: this is an empirical observation (the cooldown's optimal max-LR sat 'slightly below ... half of the optimal cosine maximum LR' in their small-scale sweep, Fig. 3) and a comparison-setup choice, NOT a stated guideline. Frame it as: 'Hagele et al. observed the cooldown's optimum max LR near ~0.5x the optimal cosine max LR and set their constant LR to half the cosine max for fair comparison; we adopt 0.5x Apertus's peak as a starting point, ideally swept.' Also flag: it concerns the plateau/peak LR of a constant+cooldown run vs cosine's peak (scope = the same schedule's max, not a cross-recipe rule), and the paper stresses the max and final LR should be set independently and swept (lines 488-489). Evidence base is 33M-360M models, not 8B CPT, so 'roughly half' is a prior to verify, not a hard rule.

## How we use it
Cite this paper for two things only: (1) the analytical justification for using a constant-LR-plus-cooldown (WSD) schedule in CPT, including that it matches well-tuned cosine, that ~20% (and for long runs even <20%) cooldown suffices, and that the (1-sqrt) decay shape beats linear; and (2) as supporting (not originating) evidence for the project's '0.5x Apertus peak LR' choice -- but reframe that choice in CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md as an empirical observation plus a fair-comparison setup, not a paper-stated guideline. Concretely: change any wording like 'the paper recommends setting max LR to half the cosine value' to 'Hagele et al. observed the cooldown's optimal max LR near ~0.5x the optimal cosine max LR (small-scale, 33M-360M) and themselves set the constant LR to half cosine's peak for fair comparison; we use 0.5x as a starting prior and should sweep it.' Carry over three caveats the doc likely omits: (a) max and final LR should be set/swept independently, and decaying fully to zero can hurt downstream metrics; (b) all evidence here is sub-360M on SlimPajama, so the 'half' and equivalence claims are unverified at 8B and for continued-pretraining-of-an-existing-model; (c) for our Greek CPT, the paper's continual-learning point is a positive: resume at the high constant LR from a pre-cooldown checkpoint to avoid re-warming spikes, and we may upweight the Greek mix during the cooldown as curriculum (Hu et al. / Llama 3 style), though the paper itself did not test mixture changes.
