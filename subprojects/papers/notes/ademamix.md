# The AdEMAMix Optimizer: Better Use of Old Gradients via a Mixture of Two First-Moment EMAs

**Source:** arXiv:2409.03137  |  **Topic:** Optimizer  |  `txt/ademamix.txt`, `pdf/ademamix.pdf`

**TL;DR.** AdEMAMix replaces Adam's single first-moment EMA with a sum of two EMAs — a fast one (beta1≈0.9) plus an alpha-scaled slow one (beta3≈0.9999) — letting the optimizer exploit very old gradients to reach lower loss and forget training data more slowly, at the cost of new hyperparameters (alpha, beta3, warmup Tα,β3) and a tendency for large beta3 to diverge early unless warmed up via schedulers.

## Key load-bearing facts
- Update rule: theta -= eta*(m1_hat + alpha*m2)/(sqrt(v_hat)+eps) + wd. m1 is the fast first-moment EMA (beta1), m2 is the slow first-moment EMA (beta3, NO bias correction), v is the second-moment EMA (beta2). beta2 is the Adam denominator EMA and is conceptually separate from beta1/beta3 (Eqs around lines 500-528, Alg.1 lines 588-664).
- Reference LLM recipe: beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, beta3=0.9999, alpha=8, Tα,β3=T (e.g. Mamba LM, line 2155). All language-modeling experiments use beta3=0.9999 (line 780). alpha in [4,10] works well in practice; sweet spots seen at alpha=10 and beta3=0.9999 for a 110M model (lines 536, 2731, 2734).
- Schedulers are needed because a large beta3 applied from step 0 causes early divergence with large-magnitude updates (lines 542-544). alpha uses a LINEAR scheduler to its final value over Tα; beta3 uses a custom scheduler (linear in t_half) from beta_start to its final value over Tβ3 (lines 545-577). beta_start is ALWAYS set to beta1 in their experiments (line 584); Tα=Tβ3=Tα,β3, typically =T (lines 583-584).
- Hyperparameter ranges giving improvements over AdamW are WIDE: in Fig.12b all tested beta3 in {0.99, 0.999, 0.9999, 0.99999} beat AdamW (only 0.999995 did not); alpha is robust across {2,6,10,15,20} (lines 2727-2734, 783-784). Tα,β3 just needs to be 'large enough to avoid early divergence' (lines 2664, 686).
- Low-iteration regime (App. C.1.5, Fig.17): for short runs (T in {32k,64k} steps), beta3=0.999 BEATS beta3=0.9999; for longer runs (T in {128k,256k}) beta3=0.9999 wins (lines 2949-3116). The method is 'best suited to settings where the number of iterations is important' (line 785-786).
- Limitations (lines 785-789): leveraging very old gradients means AdEMAMix 'can pose a problem in domains requiring fast adaptation to a sudden distribution shift' — this is a stated caveat, NOT a validated recipe; no distribution-shift experiment is run in C.1.5. The slower-forgetting property (Fig.4) is a double-edged sword.
- beta1=0 is supported to save memory (m1 replaced by raw gradient g, matching AdamW memory cost) but yields 'potentially less stable training' (lines 670-672).

## Citation audit — what CURRENT_HYPERPARAMETERS.md cites this for

**Claim:** AdEMAMix is stable only when beta2 is comfortably larger than beta1.
**Verdict:** `OVERSTATED`
**Evidence:** The paper never states a beta2-vs-beta1 stability condition. beta2 is the SECOND-moment (denominator) EMA, fixed at 0.999 in every experiment (Tables 2-4, line 2155), and is conceptually distinct from the first-moment EMAs beta1 (fast, 0.9) and beta3 (slow, ~0.9999). The design intuition is about the two FIRST-moment EMAs: 'a linear combination between a fast-changing (e.g. beta=0.9 or beta1=0) and a slow-changing (e.g. beta=0.9999) EMA' (lines 180-183), i.e. beta3 >> beta1. Stability is governed by warming beta3/alpha up via schedulers (lines 542-545), not by any beta2 relationship.
**Correction:** Rewrite to: 'AdEMAMix's design assumes the slow first-moment EMA beta3 is much larger than the fast first-moment EMA beta1 (typically beta1=0.9, beta3~0.9999); beta2 is the unchanged Adam second-moment EMA (0.999). Stability of a large beta3 is achieved by warming beta3 and alpha up from beta_start=beta1 via schedulers, not by any beta2>beta1 condition.' If the doc means beta3>beta1, say beta3, not beta2.

**Claim:** App. C.1.5 recommends beta3 = 0.999 for low-iteration / non-stationary / distribution-shift regimes.
**Verdict:** `PARTIAL`
**Evidence:** App. C.1.5 ('Impact of training for fewer iterations', lines 2949-3116) DOES support beta3=0.999 for LOW-ITERATION runs: Fig.17 shows 'using beta3=0.999 is better for T in {32k,64k}' while beta3=0.9999 wins for T in {128k,256k} (lines 3112-3116). However, C.1.5 says NOTHING about non-stationarity or distribution shift — it only varies the number of iterations at fixed token budget (17B). The distribution-shift point is a SEPARATE one-line Limitations remark (line 789: 'domains requiring fast adaptation to a sudden distribution shift') with NO experiment and NO beta3 recommendation attached.
**Correction:** Split the claim. Supported: 'App. C.1.5 / Fig.17 shows beta3=0.999 outperforms beta3=0.9999 for short runs (32k-64k steps); 0.9999 wins for longer runs.' For distribution shift: 'The paper only flags fast distribution shift as a stated limitation (Limitations, line 789); it runs no distribution-shift experiment and recommends no beta3 value for it.' Do not attribute a non-stationary/distribution-shift recommendation to C.1.5.

**Claim:** The reference alpha = 8 was tuned with beta3 = 0.9999; no alpha is published for beta3 = 0.999.
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** alpha=8 always appears paired with beta3=0.9999: the Mamba LM recipe 'beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, beta3=0.9999, ... alpha=8' (line 2155), and Fig.12b varies beta3 while holding alpha=8 at the reference beta3=0.9999 (lines 2686-2690). The published low-iteration beta3=0.999 experiments (Fig.17) keep the SAME alpha as the beta3=0.9999 run rather than re-tuning alpha for 0.999 (lines 3110-3112, 'same experimental protocol as Fig. 16'). No table or figure publishes an alpha specifically tuned at beta3=0.999; the general guidance is only alpha in [4,10] (line 536).

**Claim:** A large beta3 diverges if applied from step 0, so alpha and beta3 are warmed from beta_start = beta1 over the full run via the paper schedulers.
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** 'we still noticed AdEMAMix models using a large beta3 would diverge early ... characterized by updates of large magnitudes in the early phase. For this reason, we progressively increase the values of beta3 and alpha using schedulers' (lines 542-545). 'beta_start is always set to beta1 in our experiments' (line 584). 'Tα=Tβ3=Tα,β3 ... we typically use Tα,β3=T, with T being the total number of iterations' (lines 583-584). alpha uses a linear scheduler, beta3 a custom (t_half-linear) scheduler (lines 545-577).

**Claim:** Useful (alpha, beta3) ranges are wide.
**Verdict:** `CONFIRMED`
**Evidence:** 'Overall, we find the ranges of values of alpha, beta3 and Tα,β3 providing improvements over AdamW to be wide' (lines 783-784). Fig.12: 'wide ranges of values for which hyperparameters are outperforming the AdamW baseline' (line 2662); in (b) all beta3 in {0.99,0.999,0.9999,0.99999} beat AdamW (line 2733); in (a) alpha across {2,6,10,15,20} is robust though final loss varies (lines 2727-2731). 'the span of values resulting in good results is very large' (line 2731).

## How we use it
For Greek CPT on Apertus-8B, AdEMAMix is a candidate optimizer that can reach lower loss than AdamW and forget pretraining data more slowly (Fig.4) — a relevant property for continued pretraining where catastrophic forgetting of the base model's multilingual ability is a risk. Use the reference recipe beta1=0.9, beta2=0.999, beta3=0.9999, alpha=8 with linear alpha-warmup and the paper's beta3-scheduler, beta_start=beta1, and Tα,β3 set to (most of) the CPT step budget — large beta3 from step 0 will diverge, so the warmup is non-optional. CRITICAL caveats for THIS project: (1) CPT is typically a relatively SHORT run vs from-scratch pretraining; per App. C.1.5/Fig.17, beta3=0.999 (not 0.9999) is the better choice for low-iteration regimes (32k-64k steps) — if the Greek CPT run is short, prefer beta3=0.999 and re-tune alpha within [4,10] yourself (the paper publishes alpha=8 only for beta3=0.9999, none for 0.999). (2) AdEMAMix's reliance on very old gradients is a STATED LIMITATION for 'fast adaptation to a sudden distribution shift' (line 789) — and English-base -> Greek CPT is exactly a distribution shift; the paper provides NO experiment or recipe validating AdEMAMix in this setting, so treat it as unproven risk, not a feature. Fix the config doc's beta2/beta1 wording (it should reference beta3>beta1, the two first-moment EMAs) and remove any claim that C.1.5 recommends beta3=0.999 for distribution shift — C.1.5 only covers low iteration count.
