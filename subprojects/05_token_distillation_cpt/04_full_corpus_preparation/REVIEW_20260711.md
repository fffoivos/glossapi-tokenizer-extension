# Full CPT preparation review — 2026-07-11

## Bottom line

The existing work is broadly on the right track. The roughly 60-billion-token
data case does not depend on adding every newer GlossAPI dataset, and the chosen
training mix is directionally and empirically reasonable. The important gap is
operational: surviving components and pilot results were not yet assembled into
a source-routed, immutable full-corpus build.

Phase 04 now implements the receipt-bound CPU path through normalization,
lineage/review, cleaning, GreekMMLU decontamination, content-bound deduplication,
private/public materialization and local validation. It is still deliberately
not a completed cleaned release: source review and admission have not run, and
publication remains a separate manual gate.

### What “add the newer datasets” means

Keep the current pinned Nanochat release immutable and build a new logical
union. Use the exact first-upload `source_dataset` roster as the source-lineage
anchor, then route each current artifact as a new family, same-source
replacement or hybrid. A matching name is strong lineage evidence, but never
proof that two snapshots are byte- or document-equivalent.

- New-family rows become candidates after source-specific normalization and
  policy review.
- Same-source and hybrid rows first compete against the base at canonical
  work/document granularity. Exact matches become aliases, base-only documents
  stay, candidate-only documents may be added, and matched-but-different
  documents are selected by eligibility, privacy/decontamination status,
  extraction completeness and deterministic source precedence.
- Sectioned representations must be grouped before comparison and alternate
  text fields must never be concatenated.
- Psepheda, E-Locus and LibDUTH require explicit comparison with Nanochat's
  OpenArchives aggregate; LibIEP with school-book works; and eellak-articles
  with ellakv2 before any net-additive claim.
- Preserve both winner and loser provenance, then run post-clean exact and near
  deduplication across the complete retained union.

The production audit must publish a per-source ModernGreek-148k token waterfall
from raw input through cleaning, GreekMMLU removal, PII masking,
replacement/base retirement, exact dedup and near dedup. The 4.32B card subtotal
is a gross discovery signal, not expected training yield.

## Academic ToC and bibliography cleaning

The two-head line model is good enough to promote into an audit pipeline, not
good enough to apply globally or destructively without the remaining gates.

The held-out operating points already favor retention of prose:

| Head | Held-out recall | Prose protection | Genuine-prose lines removed |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Bibliography | 0.824 | 0.9990 | 61 / 60,814 |
| ToC | 0.756 | 0.9997 | 9 / 28,609 |

This is an acceptable adaptation/retention tradeoff for source-specific audit.
Bibliography recall should not be raised merely to remove more tokens; the
lower-cost improvements are better validation, segmentation and review.

Recommended remaining work, in order:

1. Treat every surviving annotation as **LLM silver**, never human gold. The
   existing SPAN decisions can be reconnected to receipt-bound BIB text and
   coordinates from the staged sources; they contain no ToC supervision. The
   separate 2,000-document joint ToC+BIB `STRUCT_2K` handoff has since been
   recovered and checksum-locked. Its importer excludes all 608 historical-test
   documents before creating the new train/validation source. No import or
   ladder job has run, and no replacement human corpus is needed.
2. Add Pergamos to a fresh source-balanced validation sample and report span
   IoU/document bootstrap intervals, not only line metrics.
3. Run exact original/bibliography-only/ToC-only/combined tokenization on every
   routed source; review overlaps, emptied documents, loss-fraction strata,
   random affected rows and blind negatives.
4. Keep the bibliography operating point conservative. Consider ToC feature or
   front-gate refinements only after reviewing its false negatives by source.
5. For any future application run, freeze the approved structural policy before
   Stage10, then require Stage54 plus the independent manual deletion-safety
   audit of exactly 100 predictions (50 ToC, 50 bibliography). This audit is a
   false-deletion gate, not training annotation.
6. Do not add a neural sequence model unless the source-stratified audit shows a
   material residual failure. The current bottleneck is validation and pipeline
   integration, not model capacity.

### Where the structural remover belongs

| Source family | Policy |
|---|---|
| Greek PhD, OpenArchives, canonical Kallipos/Pergamos | Apply only after parity, exact token audit and review |
| Psepheda, E-Locus, LibDUTH, LibIEP | Shadow audit first |
| Raw sectioned books/serials | Segment/group first, then shadow audit |
| HPLT, Diavgeia, legal text, news, blogs, parliament, dialogue, comments, subtitles | Disabled |

There is no defensible reason to run an academic bibliography/ToC model over
all non-academic text. A source allowlist is both safer and cheaper.

## How many tokens could structural cleaning remove?

The only surviving corpus-scale sizing signal is from the older academic
detector counters: roughly 3.11B candidate characters for Greek PhD and 3.47B
for OpenArchives, or 6.58B characters combined. A crude conversion suggests
about 1.6–1.8B tokens, roughly 3% of a 60B pool.

That is not an answer suitable for policy. The old header-to-end detector could
overrun into appendices or substantive text, and it did not measure ToC and
bibliography as exact full-text counterfactuals. Treat 1.6–1.8B only as a CPU and
storage sizing estimate. `structural_token_loss.py` now computes the exact loss
with the pinned ModernGreek-148k tokenizer, including non-additive boundary and
bibliography/ToC interaction effects. The source payload is now present on
Clariden, but the exact result remains unavailable until normalization and the
receipt-bound audit run complete. The current CPT policy applies no structural
deletion regardless of that sizing estimate.

## Diavgeia needs a different cleaner

A 400-row sample of the pinned Diavgeia release found the Ministry signing
footer in 400/400 rows and no ToC/bibliography-heading signal. Overlapping flags
included 58 `privateData=true` rows, 150 AFM candidates, 157 phone/email
candidates, 15 identity-document candidates, 20 personnel-table candidates and
16 corrected-version records.

Recommended Diavgeia order:

1. Emit exact reversible spans for the complete signing footer and isolated
   `ΑΔΑ:` watermark lines; measure their exact token delta.
2. Initially exclude `privateData=true` pending a separate token/report review.
3. Mask validated direct identifiers (AFM, email, phone, identity numbers) with
   typed placeholders; do not apply generic person-name NER globally.
4. Quarantine PII-heavy personnel tables for review.
5. Link corrected/superseded decisions and choose the appropriate version rather
   than counting both blindly.
6. Normalize variable identifiers only for template fingerprints, then
   deduplicate or cap dominant organization/decision-type template groups.
7. Preserve statutes, legal citations, recitals, agendas and substantive
   administrative prose.

Stage50 consumes the reviewed candidate ledgers and implements narrow
source/PII actions with exact accounting. Whether each Diavgeia action is
admitted still depends on the redacted source review and token waterfall; the
academic structural cleaner remains disabled for this source.

## Whole-corpus CPT order

1. Verify the already staged exact source revisions with a fresh existing-only
   acquisition receipt.
2. Normalize every source once into a canonical schema that preserves source,
   document ID, upstream revision and original hash. Never concatenate alternate
   text representations.
3. Apply the existing Apertus-overlap overlay to the Nanochat base; compare only
   genuinely new/replacement sources against it and freeze a pre-transform
   source-key/exact-identity graph.
4. Build a redacted packet of at least 100 documents per exact
   `source_dataset`; review it on the authenticated Mac with resumable Codex
   `gpt-5.6-luna` (`low`, batch 12, two workers), then checksum-confirm source
   admission.
5. Stage50 applies source decisions, source-specific cleaning and
   high-confidence PII masking. If required, review a new packet from only the
   post-clean source datasets and freeze terminal admission.
6. Always run Stage58. In the current `audit_only` run it records a no-op and
   preserves Stage50 text; only a separately pre-authorized policy plus Stage54
   could apply ToC/BIB spans last.
7. Freeze/decontaminate GreekMMLU, then run post-transform exact deduplication
   and within-/cross-family near deduplication. Finish with another exact check.
8. Re-tokenize the final retained corpus, publish the per-rule/source token
   waterfall, solve the training weights against realized token counts, and
   materialize immutable shards plus quarantine and provenance manifests.
9. Optionally publish the manually gated, new-empty redistributable delta. Its
   exact remote inventory is the checksum-bound `README.md`, validated
   `data/**/*.parquet`, and explicit `provenance/**`; it is not the full private
   training corpus.
10. Build Megatron binaries from the private release; do not make Megatron
    preprocessing part of source cleaning.

## Clariden execution

Clariden does not expose a CPU-only production partition for project `a0140`.
Use the CPU side of `normal` GH200 allocations without GPU/GRES requests, keep
roughly 256 cores busy for corpus audits, and reserve `debug` for bounded smokes.
The shared `xfer` nodes are for internal CSCS filesystem transfer, not external
HF downloads or corpus processing.

Runtime installation and detector compilation are also Slurm setup jobs. The
Mac and login node remain coordination surfaces. Full jobs use bounded
files/shards with atomic receipts or checkpoints and must still fit the 12-hour
allocation. Resume only through the documented run/stage path; reused artifacts
are revalidated before acceptance.
