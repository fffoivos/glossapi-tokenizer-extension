# Worst-document review for `position_hist_component_scope` (2026-07-22)

## Scope

This review covers the ten sealed-test documents with the most trusted line
errors (`FP + FN`) in each source: 10 Greek PhD, 10 Kallipos and 10
OpenArchives documents. Labels are dual-annotator consensus silver, not human
gold. Selection and metrics are reproduced directly from the frozen
`position_hist_component_scope` prediction and probability arrays.

The interactive reader is generated by
`sequence_models/bibliography_nextgen_worst_docs_site.py`. Its reviewed
document-level interpretations are stored separately in
`sequence_models/bibliography_nextgen_worst_docs_analysis_20260722.json`.

## Main findings

1. **Whole valid components are still vetoed.** Several clean bibliographies
   with explicit Markdown headings are missed in full or in large part. The
   clearest examples are Greek PhD `dab1ddd9d739...`, Kallipos
   `e7abf3e1eecb...`, and OpenArchives `0d9bc88ee44a...` and
   `0169eea0b476...`. This is not primarily a line-feature failure.
2. **Citation-shaped material outside bibliography sections remains the main
   precision problem.** Footnotes, citation-formatting examples, CV/research
   histories, metadata tables and ordinary enumerated teaching lists can form
   accepted components.
3. **Hard lower boundaries need strengthening.** OpenArchives
   `09172b1ae1f...` and `36994f1c46b...` run from a bibliography into figure or
   appendix material. Appendix/next-chapter headings should be strong stops.
4. **Webographies need collective treatment.** Bare URLs and multiline
   title/URL/access-date entries are weak individually but unambiguous under an
   electronic-sources heading.
5. **Some apparent failures are evaluation-policy problems.** Consensus labels
   include CV publication/conference lists and scattered body footnotes in
   several documents. We should decide explicitly whether the production
   cleaner is intended to remove those. OCR filler and image markers inside
   silver blocks also inflate line-error counts without representing missed
   citation content.
